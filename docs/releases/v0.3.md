# WingFoil Benchmark v0.3 — WingFoil as designed, harnesses as configured

**Date:** 2026-11-20
**Release element:** [rel-v0-3](../memory/release/rel-v0-3.md) · **Plan:** [plan-005](../plans/plan-005-release-v0-3.md)

v0.3 measures **WingFoil 0.3.1 as it is designed to be used**: the agent drives its workflow engine and stops at each
review gate for the approver (step A), where v0.2's arm carried the process by hand. It adds a **configured variant**
of every harness, set up for the project as a team would, and an **independent review loop** after every run,
measured in every arm. The scenarios move to version **1.1**, scored by content checks v2. Outside the reference
campaign, v0.3 also runs the benchmark under a second agent, **Codex**. Its results are **preliminary**: only S1 ran
three times per arm, and no claim of statistical significance is made.

- **Results:** <https://wingfoil.github.io/wingfoil-benchmark/1f70fcaf2ec2/1/>, the reference campaign. The other
  executions of v0.3 and those of v0.1 and v0.2 stay at their addresses, listed on
  <https://wingfoil.github.io/wingfoil-benchmark/>.
- **How the benchmark works:** the [method page](https://wingfoil.github.io/wingfoil-benchmark/1f70fcaf2ec2/1/method.html)
  states every rule, including the ones that limit what the results can show.
- **Transcripts** of every agent session, with the harness artifacts the runs installed: `transcripts.tar.gz` (540
  transcripts, sha256 `8d7479f1…934d99`) and `harnesses.tar.gz` (sha256 `d195fc2d…76492c`), assets of the release
  [`1f70fcaf2ec2-1`](https://github.com/wingfoil/wingfoil-benchmark/releases/tag/1f70fcaf2ec2-1).

## What is compared

The same agent and model work through the same tasks in thirteen arms. Only the harness around the agent changes:

- **baseline**: the agent, the starting project and a short operating manual;
- **wingfoil**: WingFoil **v0.3.1** (`870bd440`) at **step A**: the session agent drives WingFoil's workflow, acting in
  each step's role and stopping at each review gate, where the approver's verdict comes back through WingFoil;
- **speckit**: GitHub's Spec Kit **v1.1.0**, through its skills;
- **openspec**: OpenSpec **1.14.0**, through its commands;
- **baseline-docs**, **speckit-docs**, **openspec-docs**: each harness's docs control, the same information as one
  plain Markdown file without the tool; baseline-docs carries every WingFoil role's rules;
- **the configured variants**, new: `wingfoil-configured`, `speckit-configured` and `openspec-configured`, and their
  docs controls `baseline-docs-configured`, `speckit-docs-configured` and `openspec-docs-configured`. A configuring
  agent wrote each harness's configuration for the project once per scenario version, in the tool's own configuration
  places, without seeing a step's prompt or the oracle; each configured docs control gets that configuration's
  information as text. A plain arm measures a harness as its init leaves it; a configured arm, once it is set up for
  the project.

**The review loop**, new: after each run an independent reviewer reads the change, and a fixer acts on its findings,
for up to three rounds. Its rounds (M-V1), the findings a fix induced (M-V2) and its cost (M-V3) are measured apart from
the run's cost, which stays comparable with v0.2's.

**Pins:** Claude Code 2.1.300, model `claude-sonnet-5-5` at effort `medium` (a `claude-haiku-5-5` slice), approver
policy v5. Costs are API-equivalent, at 0.8917 EUR/USD, whatever the actual billing: the agent ran on a subscription.

How to read it:

- **Correctness is near the ceiling in every arm.** S1's and S8's hidden tests are at their maximum in every arm
  but one run (S1's openspec r1, 150/165). The arms differ on S2's and S3's checks and on M-F1.
- **WingFoil at step A** keeps the most checks (19/20; with its configured variant, the only arms at 3/3 on S2's
  checks) at 1.83 times the baseline's cost and 1.46 times its docs control's. On S3 it loses two hidden tests and
  one decision on Sonnet against the baseline (one run).
- **Configuring a harness does not change WingFoil's results**, and costs a little more. It helps OpenSpec on S3
  (the only arm at 5/5 with the check), and lowers Spec Kit on S2 and S3 (0/3 on S2's checks, 30/37 and M-F1 2/5 on
  S3, one run each).
- **Written rules alone still give most of the checks' gain**: baseline-docs reaches 18/20 at 1.27 times the baseline.

**The weak model** (Haiku 5.5, S1 and S3, one run each): with WingFoil, S3 at 37/37 and M-F1 5/5 against the
baseline's 35/37 and 3/5, where on Sonnet WingFoil loses on the same scenario; S1 at the maximum in both arms. Under
Codex, campaign-007 read the same direction on its weak model, `gpt-6-luna` (n = 3).

**The review loop:** 82 loops, 8.50 € in all. 55 were clean at round 1, 18 took two rounds and 8 three; 21 of the 31
findings after round 1 were induced by a fix. One loop ended failed, its reviewer's findings unread
([bug-055](../memory/bug/bug-055-a-reviewer-s-findings-after-prose-unfenced-end-the-review-loop-as-failed-and-its-should-fix-never-reaches-a-fixer.md)).
Mean rounds range from 1.00 (baseline-docs-configured) to 2.00 (speckit-configured); wingfoil 1.50, the baseline 1.25.

## The hypotheses written before v0.3

[Calibration v0.3](../calibration/v0.3.md) §6 restated v0.1's hypotheses on the engine, and the reference campaign read
them on Sonnet 5.5:

- **Hold:** H4 (S2's checks 3/3), H5 (S8's rules and hidden tests), H7 (the engine declared, and no scenario that
  requires it an expected failure) and H11 (WingFoil gains more on the weak model, on 2 of 3 readings, n = 1 on Haiku).
- **Fail:** H1 (S1 at 1.80×, above the 1.5× target; the ratio falls on 2 of 4 scenarios from v0.2), H2 (turns on S1,
  2.71× against 1.8×), H3 (M-F1 3/5 on S3), H8 (1.46× the docs control, against 1.1×) and H10 (the checks not
  saturated: S2 11 and S3 12 of 13 arms below the maximum).
- **Not read:** H6 against v0.1 (other scenario versions; S3's wingfoil run is two tests below the baseline) and H9
  (all scenarios are one request per session).

The engine's own effect on cost, the question v0.2 left open, is now measured: on these scenarios driving WingFoil's
workflow costs more than carrying its process by hand, and its configured variant does not lower it.

## Known limits

- **Preliminary:** S2, S3 and S8 ran once per arm, and so did the Haiku slice. Only S1 has three runs. No difference
  is claimed as significant.
- **Saturation:** on these scenarios Sonnet 5.5 is near the ceiling in every arm. The harnesses differ on cost and
  checks more than on correctness.
- **Small steps:** the scenarios' steps are small, so a process harness's cost at each gate weighs more than it would
  on a task of real size.
- **One request per session:** a harness's gates and roles matter most across a project's life; only S10, in
  campaign-008, exercises them.
- **The comparison with v0.2** changes the manual, the scenario version and the approver policy; it is read as a
  chain.
- **The configurations** were written once per scenario version and harness (n = 1): each configured arm measures one
  configuration, not the best one.
- **Benchmark by WingFoil's maintainer** (threat T1): the questions, metrics and hypotheses were fixed before any run,
  and losses are published like wins.

## Changes in the benchmark since v0.2

- campaign presets and `extends:`, and a second agent, Codex, with its own adapter, price table and approver policy;
- the method corrected: content checks v2, the scenarios at 1.1, v0.2's campaigns re-scored offline;
- the wingfoil arm at step A, WingFoil's workflow driven by the agent, with an approver hook that carries verdicts
  through WingFoil;
- the configured variants: a configuring agent, its sessions recorded and scanned, the configured docs controls;
- the review loop and its metrics M-V1, M-V2 and M-V3;
- the site's catalogue and its across-agents page.

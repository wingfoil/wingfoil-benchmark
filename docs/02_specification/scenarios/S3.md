## 2. Rationale

S3 carries the benchmark's central claim: a harness should let **fresh sessions** respect decisions
taken earlier, revise them explicitly when a change request contradicts them, and recall a rule that
was stated once and never implemented.

Each step runs in a new session. Only the repository carries the decisions from one step to the
next.

## 3. Seed

- An empty TypeScript project, as in S1, whose README describes a windsurf school that rents boards
  and sails.
- **Must not contain:** any rental code, or any statement of the decisions below. Those are given
  only in step 1.

## 4. Required entry points

Exported from `src/index.ts`, and introduced by the step that needs them:

| Step | Export | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `createRentalService()` | returns a service object with in-memory storage |
| 1 | `service.addEquipment(item)`, `service.quote(request)`, `service.book(request)` | inventory, price quote, booking |
| 2 | `service.availability(query)` | free equipment in a period |
| 5 | `service.cancel(bookingId, at)` | cancellation, returning the refund |

The request and response shapes are specified in the prompt of the step that introduces them.
Everything else is free.

## 5. Decisions under test

Step 1 states these decisions. They are the oracle's list for M-F1.

| ID | Decision | Tested in |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Money is an **integer number of euro cents**. | every step |
| D2 | Times are **UTC**, as ISO-8601 strings. | every step |
| D3 | A booking covers **whole days only**. | revised in step 4 |
| D4 | The same item cannot be booked twice in overlapping periods. | steps 1, 2, 4 |
| D5 | **Cancellation policy:** a cancellation less than 24 hours before the start gets no refund; otherwise the full amount is refunded. It is **not implemented in step 1**, and the prompt says it will be needed later. | step 5 only |

## 6. Steps

| # | Intent | The prompt must say |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Initial build: inventory, quotes, bookings | D1–D5 and the step-1 entry points. It says that D5 is not to be implemented yet. It says nothing about recording decisions. |
| 2 | Availability query | the `availability` contract |
| 3 | Multi-day discount: 10% off rentals of 3 days or more | the rule, and nothing that restates D1–D5 |
| 4 | **Contradicting change:** hourly rentals with an hourly price | the new rule, and nothing about D3. The expected response revises D3 **explicitly** and keeps whole-day bookings working. |
| 5 | **Recall:** add cancellations with refunds | the `cancel` contract, and **not** the policy. The expected response applies D5. |

## 7. Oracle design

**Public oracle:**

- Hidden functional tests per step, on the per-step snapshot: quotes, bookings, availability,
  discounts, hourly rentals, cancellations.
- **M-F1 decision checks,** scripted:
  - D1 and D2: every monetary value and every time the API returns has the declared form;
  - D4: overlapping bookings are rejected, including when whole-day and hourly bookings are mixed
    after step 4;
  - D3: after step 4, whole-day bookings behave as before (outcome check), **and** the revision is
    recorded somewhere in the repository (content check, format-neutral). A silent change counts
    as a failure.
  - D5: refunds after step 5 follow the policy stated in step 1.
- **M-F2:** cost and pass rate of steps 2–5.

**Hold-out additions:**

- extra boundary tests (the exact 24-hour limit, the 3-day threshold, day boundaries in UTC);
- a mixed day/hourly overlap matrix.

## 8. Capability requirements

None required. Keeping decisions across sessions is exactly what is measured. WingFoil 0.1.0 can
record decisions with `memory add`, but it cannot move them through their states. That shows up as
extra work, not as an expected failure.

## 9. Scenario-specific threats

- **The step-1 prompt hints at recording:** the phrase "it will be needed later" is the only hint,
  and it is the same in every arm. It tells the agent that the information matters, not how to keep
  it.
- **The D3 content check favours verbose arms:** the check requires only that some record of the
  revision exists, anywhere in the repository. The amount of text is not scored.

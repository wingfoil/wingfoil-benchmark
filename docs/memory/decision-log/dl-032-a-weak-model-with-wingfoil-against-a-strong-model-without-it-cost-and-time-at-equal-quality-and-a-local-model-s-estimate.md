## Context

The approver (chat, 2026-10-29), after campaign-007's results: one of WingFoil's aims is that **weak or local models
deliver the quality of frontier models**; campaign-007 suggests it on Luna, but the advantage of a cheaper model is
lost if, with WingFoil, it takes too much time or too many tokens. How is that validated?

What the benchmark has: every comparison is within one model (T14: "cross-model numbers are shown as a separate
comparison, never mixed with same-model ones"); M-K1 (tokens, API-equivalent cost) and M-K2 (wall time, turns,
interventions) per run; no metric reads a weak model's harness arm against a strong model's baseline, and nothing
estimates what a local model would take for the same work.

**A zero-cost first reading** of the executions that have both models (generated by a script over the runs'
`score.json` and steps' `usage.json`, 2026-10-29). Agent time is the sessions' own (tool time included); the local
profiles are **illustrative assumptions** (prefill and decode speeds), not measurements, and the local times count
the model's work only.

Read as preliminary (n = 1 but S1, every cell):

- **On Codex, the claim holds so far:** Luna with WingFoil matches Terra without it (573/579 against 567/579;
  hold-out 175/185 against 176/185) at about a twelfth of the cost and in less agent time (31 against 50 min).
- **On Claude Code, it does not, in time:** Haiku with v0.2's manual (S1 and S3 only) is level with Sonnet's
  baseline on quality (195/202 and 63/65 hold-out, against 199/202 and 61/65) at 0.39 of the cost, but takes 19
  minutes against 5, and processes about ten times the tokens.
- **The tokens are the local risk:** the wingfoil arm processes 2.4 times (Codex, Luna against Terra's baseline) to
  10 times (Claude Code, Haiku against Sonnet's) the tokens, nearly all re-read context that the APIs price at a
  fraction through their cache. Without a prefix cache a local model pays every one of them: at the mid profile
  about 3.7 hours of model time for Luna's sample against 2.0 for Terra's baseline.

## Options

- **A. A cross-model parity comparison, measured** (proposed): a separate comparison, within one agent, of a weak
  model's harness arm against a strong model's baseline, with verdicts on quality, cost, time and tokens, and a
  local estimate from the tokens; implemented in the scorer, read on every execution that has both models.
- **B. The analysis by hand** at each campaign, as above, with no metric: quick, but its figures are not
  reproducible from the results and the method page cannot state it.
- **C. Wait for a local model** (an agent adapter, v0.4 or after) and measure there only: the question stays open
  until then, and the token overhead is not known before it matters.

## Proposal

**A.**

1. **The hypothesis, stated (H-W):** within one agent, a weak model with a harness reaches the quality of a strong
   model without it at a lower cost and in less time. It is read per scenario and over a matched sample (the same
   scenarios and repetitions in both cells).
2. **The comparison, separate (T14):** "weak with harness against strong without", marked cross-model, never mixed
   with the same-model controls; its own section of `aggregate.json` and of the execution's page. Its cells: the
   weak model's harness arm, the strong model's baseline, and, for reference, the weak model's baseline and docs
   control and the strong model's harness arm.
3. **The verdicts:** quality (M-Q1 final and hold-out, and the scenario's checks) **not worse beyond the variance**;
   then cost (M-K1), time (M-K2 wall and agent time) and tokens (fresh, cached, output) as ratios to the strong
   baseline. **H-W holds** for a scenario only if quality is not worse and both cost and time are lower; tokens are
   reported, not judged (their price is the agent's).
4. **The local estimate, labelled an estimate:** each cell's model time at declared profiles (prefill and decode
   speeds; with and without a prefix cache), from its tokens; the profiles named on the method page as assumptions,
   to be replaced by measurements once a local model runs. It answers "how fast must a local machine be for the
   weak model to keep its advantage?".
5. **The sample:** a verdict beyond "preliminary" needs n ≥ 3 per scenario in both cells (v0.2's retrospective
   item 3 sets the number for every campaign). campaign-007 gives Luna n = 3 everywhere but Terra n = 1 outside S1;
   W17's Claude Code campaign (dl-031, next step 1) runs Haiku and Sonnet, the baseline and the step-A arm, every
   scenario × 3, so that it answers H-W on both agents.
6. **Where the tokens go:** a task reads the wingfoil arm's transcripts for what its extra tokens are (directives
   loaded, documents re-read, the approval round trips' resumes) and files what WingFoil could cut as feedback notes
   (the inbox, dl-163); every token saved weighs most on a local model.
7. **A task implements 2–4** (scorer, `aggregate.json`, the execution's page and the method page; red-first; no
   real-agent session, 0 €), and re-scores campaign-007 and campaign-002 with it; the figures above are then the
   scorer's, not this script's.

## Consequences

- WingFoil's weak-model claim becomes a measured, per-scenario verdict with its cost and time, on both agents once
  W17's campaign runs; the governance claim with strong models (dl-029) stays a separate question.
- The local question gets a number before any local model runs: the speed a machine needs; a local agent adapter
  (dl-031, next step 3) is then planned against it.
- Not in this decision: a local model's adapter and eligibility; the number of repetitions for every campaign (v0.2's
  retrospective item 3); any publication of the cross-model figures before the task's scorer computes them.

## 3. Measured cost per run (Sonnet 5, the counted dry runs)

| Scenario | baseline | baseline-docs | wingfoil | Runs in the campaign |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 (5 steps) | 1.91 € | 2.14 € | 4.41 € | 3 per arm |
| S2 | 0.94 € | 1.41 € | 1.42 € | 1 per arm |
| S3 | 0.84 € | 1.97 € | 2.29 € | 1 per arm |
| S8 | 1.05 € | 1.48 € | 2.21 € | 1 per arm |

- The mean is about **1.8 € a run**, against the 1.2 € K4 sized the scenarios for. The wingfoil arm costs most
  everywhere: 1.5× to 2.7× the baseline, with 1.5× to 2.5× its turns (S1: 215 turns against 87); it reads
  its directives and decisions and records tasks and decision-logs at every step.
- The setup cost (M-K3) is **0** in every run: setups are scripted and spend no agent tokens, so M-K4 (break-even)
  is the special case `rel-v0-1` W9 anticipated — there is no setup to pay back, and the harness's overhead sits in
  each step's cost.
- **Opus 5 costs about 5–7× Sonnet 5** on the same run: S1 baseline 13.02 € and stopped at its cap during step 5,
  against 1.91 € (6.8×); S1 wingfoil 21.31 €, completed, against 4.41 € (4.8×). That is 2.5× the per-token price
  and 3.6× (baseline: 12.7 M against 3.5 M) or 2.1× (wingfoil: 24.9 M against 11.8 M) the tokens — the cost per
  token rose less than the list price (1.9× and 2.3×), the share of cheap cache reads differing. Opus scored every pair of S1 in the
  wingfoil arm (165/165, 48/48).

### Where the wingfoil arm's cost goes

Read from the transcripts of the counted wingfoil dry runs (7, 10, 14, 17 on Sonnet 5; 19 on Opus 5), each assistant
message priced by its own usage and classed by the tools it called:

| Share of the run's cost | S1 | S2 | S3 | S8 | S1 on Opus |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Orientation: `wingfoil directives list --role developer`, `memory search`, reading directives and decisions (about five messages a step) | 12 % | 18 % | 19 % | 18 % | 9 % |
| Recording: tasks and decision-logs added, written, submitted, approved | 23 % | 15 % | 24 % | 23 % | 19 % |
| The work itself | 65 % | 67 % | 57 % | 58 % | 72 % |
| — of which exploring the code before the first change to `src/` | 11 % | 35 % | 12 % | 15 % | — |
| Approval round trips: the part of each step after the neutral approver's reply | 10 % | 7 % | 9 % | 8 % | 5 % |

- **No MCP call** in any run: the agent read through the CLI, as the manual maps it; the `developer-session` MCP
  Prompt, which inlines the role's directive bodies, is never loaded by Claude Code on its own.
- **The developer's directives weigh about 1 300 tokens** (S8: seven bound to the role and three global, of fourteen):
  injected at a session's start they would cost about 0.01 USD a step in cache reads, against the orientation
  messages they would replace.
- **The process the arm runs is partly the manual's choice.** One task per request, one decision-log per decision,
  each submitted and approved through the neutral approver, is what `arms/wingfoil/manual.md` prescribes over the
  `Kanban` template the setup initialises (adr-003); WingFoil does not impose it. It represents a disciplined use of
  WingFoil, and the method page is to say so. A lighter manual could be measured on v0.2.2 already; a template
  WingFoil itself publishes for agents would be adopted by the arm, whose setup follows the harness's documentation
  (T12).

**Where the cost could go, by lever** — estimates from the shares above, not measurements. The orientation 80 %
removed by role context injected at a session's start (WingFoil v0.3's `agent execute` with the `{role}-session`
Prompt carrying the role's directives and relevance-filtered Memory, in its backlog); a third of the code's
rediscovery removed by linked sessions (a quarter on S2, where exploring is the bug hunt itself; WingFoil v0.4's
`--ref` summary handoff); half the recording and most approval round trips removed by a workflow designed for
agents (one record per step, approval by risk):

| Mean wingfoil run (Sonnet 5) | Cost | Against the baseline | Against baseline-docs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Today, WingFoil v0.2.2 | 2.58 € | 2.2× | 1.45× |
| + role context at a session's start | about 2.26 € (−12 %) | about 1.9× | about 1.25× |
| + linked sessions | about 2.13 € (−17 %) | about 1.8× | about 1.2× |
| + a workflow designed for agents | about 1.70–1.85 € (−28 to −34 %) | about 1.45–1.55× | about 0.95–1.05× |

The recording carries what the wingfoil arm wins on (S2's checks, S3's D3 and M-F1): a lever that cuts its content
rather than its mechanics would lose that, so every cost hypothesis below is paired with one on continuity. Running
WingFoil v0.3 as it is meant to be used also means WingFoil launching the agent (`agent execute`), which the runner
does today: an arm where the harness launches the agent is a change to the runner's requirements, for the
benchmark's v0.2.

## 8. Difficulty and registration

| Scenario | Difficulty on Sonnet 5 | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | saturated with 4 steps (every arm 135/135, 33/33); with **step 5, `createPatch`** (S1 1.3), one arm off the maximum (baseline-docs 158/165, 43/48). Step 5's length bound in effect asks for a shift-aware array diff (an LCS): baseline-docs diffed arrays index by index, and lost seven pairs whose index-wise patch, though far from the whole document, passes the bound — among them a one-item insertion into a three-item array. The prompt does not say so; S1's step-5 scores are to be read that way | made harder (the approver, 2026-10-02); registered as 1.0 with 5 steps |
| S2 | every arm at the maximum on hidden tests; the arms differ on its checks | registered as is |
| S3 | every arm 34–35/37; the arms differ on D3 and M-F1 | registered as is |
| S8 | the arms differ on its rules and, for wingfoil, on hidden tests | registered as is |

**Registered:** S1@1.0 (hash in its dry runs 15–17), S2@1.0, S3@1.0, S8@1.0. From here a change to any of them is
a new version (REQ-FMT-09); a version freezes for good at its first stored campaign result.

**A saturation rule, applied here to S1 by hand.** A scenario whose hidden tests every arm passes in full stops
telling the arms apart on correctness; it is then made harder as a new version, or kept unchanged as an anchor that
later model and harness generations are run against. S1 was the first case, found by these dry runs; S2 and S8 are at
their maximum on hidden tests already and differ on their checks only. Agents able to take more work and more
complexity at once will saturate today's scenarios further: the rule, the anchors, and the level of delegation a
scenario tests (§11) are proposed as a decision-log for the benchmark's next specification.

## 11. Hypotheses for v0.2

Written before v0.2 runs, at the approver's request (2026-10-02), as the defence against T1: the benchmark's
maintainer also builds WingFoil, so what WingFoil's workflow engine is expected to change is stated here, where
v0.2's results can confirm or refute it, instead of being read into them afterwards. Each hypothesis compares
the wingfoil arm with WingFoil and its workflow engine against the same arm on v0.2.2 (this calibration and the
v0.1 campaign), on the same registered scenario versions, the same model and agent, and against the baseline of
the same execution. They are predictions, not targets; each holds or fails as written.

| # | Hypothesis | Measured by | Today (v0.2.2, calibration, n = 1) | Holds when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | The engine lowers the process's overhead: the wingfoil arm's cost relative to the baseline falls (§7: about −12 % from role context at a session's start, about −17 % with linked sessions) | M-K1, wingfoil ÷ baseline, per scenario | S1 2.31×, S2 1.51×, S3 2.73×, S8 2.10× | the ratio falls on at least three of the four scenarios, and below 1.5× on S1 |
| H2 | Fewer turns spent finding the process | turns per run, wingfoil ÷ baseline | S1 215 ÷ 87 = 2.5× | the ratio falls on S1 |
| H3 | Continuity holds or improves | M-F1 on S3; D3's check | 4/5; D3 recorded | M-F1 ≥ 4/5 and D3 recorded, in every wingfoil run |
| H4 | Recall across sessions holds | S2's checks, the duplicate and the false report | 3/3 | 3/3 in every wingfoil run |
| H5 | A review phase catches what a read rule does not | S8: M-E1 and its hidden tests | 16/16 rule checks; 20/23 hidden, 5/7 hold-out | 16/16 kept, and hidden tests at the baseline-docs arm's level or above |
| H6 | Correctness is unchanged | M-Q1 final, public and hold-out, every scenario | at the baseline's level or within one test, except S8 | no scenario falls below its v0.1 wingfoil value by more than one test |
| H7 | No expected failure for the engine's gap | REQ-SCO-10, the wingfoil arm's `provides` | `workflow-engine: false` (T10); no v0.1 scenario needs it | `workflow-engine: true` once re-assessed, and any v0.2 scenario that requires it is not an expected failure |
| H8 | A workflow designed for agents (one record per step, approval by risk) brings the wingfoil arm's cost to baseline-docs' **and** keeps continuity | M-K1, wingfoil ÷ baseline-docs; M-F1 on S3; S2's checks | 1.45×; 4/5; 3/3 | at most 1.1× baseline-docs, with M-F1 ≥ 4/5 and S2's checks 3/3 |
| H9 | The harness's advantage grows with the level of delegation: from one request a step (L1, today's scenarios) to a project over many sessions (L3) in the same scenario family | M-F1 (and checks) per euro, wingfoil against the baseline, the same family at L1 and L3 | L1 only: M-F1 4/5 against 3/5, at 2.7× the cost (S3) | the wingfoil arm's continuity per euro, against the baseline's, is higher at L3 than at L1 |
| H10 | More capable agents saturate today's L1 scenarios on their checks too, not only on hidden tests | S2's, S3's and S8's checks with the next model generation | arms differ on checks (§7) | every arm at the maximum on S2's, S3's and S8's checks |

**What would refute the claim:** H1 and H2 failing together while H3–H5 hold would say the engine adds structure
without lowering cost; H3 or H4 failing would say it disturbs what the memory life cycle already gives. A v0.2
scenario whose process needs gates and roles is needed for the engine's own value to show; S1–S3 and S8 test
that it costs less and loses nothing.

**Levels of delegation.** L1: one request a step (S1–S3, S8); L2: a whole feature the agent plans and breaks down
(S6, v0.3); L3: a project over many sessions with few instructions (M1–M3, v1.0); L4: several agents with roles and
handoffs (S9, v1.0). H9 asks for the same scenario family run at two levels, so that where the harness starts to pay
is seen on one task, not across different ones.

**The neutral control.** These hypotheses, like the v0.1 results, rest on scenarios designed alongside WingFoil. Their
control is a field WingFoil's authors did not design: a sample of a third-party benchmark's tasks, drawn with a
published seed, run by this runner in the three arms with the same pins, setup and manual, scored by its own oracle.
Such benchmarks are mostly single tasks in one session (L1), so WingFoil v0.2.2 is expected to cost more there at the
same correctness; the claim holds if it does no worse on correctness there and wins only where continuity matters.
Which suite (licence, containers, contamination T5, lookup T13) is not chosen here; at the measured costs, 30
instances in three arms cost about 100–200 €. Two further steps: our multi-session scenarios with hold-out oracles
written by third parties (F7.3, brought forward), and this benchmark run by someone else from its public pins and
campaign files.
